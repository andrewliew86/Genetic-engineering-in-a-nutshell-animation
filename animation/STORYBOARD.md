# Life, rewritten — 60-second silent cartoon

Logline: A little bacterium survives a phage encounter; the tools of that ancient struggle become tools for making human insulin.

World: flat hand-painted microbes on paper, cool blue pools becoming warm cream. Mint bacterium, violet phage, coral DNA, gold plasmid. Characters and molecular actions are explicitly illustrative, not to scale. No specific origin date is assigned to restriction enzymes or plasmids.

Motif: the gold DNA ring carries the viewer from a natural plasmid into recombinant DNA and finally a medicine-making cell.

Character arc: curious → startled → relieved → curious → helpful → proud. The phage is a mischievous visitor, not a villain with intentions.

## Shots and reading time

1. **0–9 s · Deep time.** Paper reveal. A large mint bacterium bobs peacefully. A violet phage notices it, anticipates, lands, and injects a coral DNA strand. The bacterium's smile becomes a surprised mouth. Reads: 0–2 world/characters; 2–4 approach; 4–6 attachment; 6–8.5 DNA enters. Brush transition into a close-up.
   Caption: “Long before humans, bacteria faced tiny invaders: viruses called bacteriophages.”
2. **9–19 s · An ancient arms race.** Invading DNA spans the cell interior. A gold enzyme grips it, holds, cuts, and the pieces drift apart. The bacterium relaxes. Reads: 9–11 DNA; 11–13 recognition; 13–14 cut; 14–18 relief and protected host DNA. Wipe to the same cell.
   Caption: “Some bacteria evolved restriction enzymes: proteins that cut invading DNA. Chemical marks help protect their own DNA.”
3. **19–28 s · Nature’s toolkit.** The bacterium reveals a small gold ring inside itself. A second ring draws in and separates. Reads: 19–21 ring; 21–25 copying; 25–27 two copies. Gold ring carries through into next shot.
   Caption: “Bacteria also carry plasmids: extra DNA rings that can copy themselves. Scientists saw a useful carrier.”
4. **28–39 s · 1973.** The enlarged gold plasmid opens; a coral gene segment moves into the gap; two small seals join it. The ring shrinks and enters the bacterium. Reads: 28–30 opened ring; 30–33 insert; 33–35 joining; 35–38 entry and proud reaction. Paint wipe.
   Caption: “In 1973, scientists joined DNA pieces into working plasmids. Bacteria could copy the new instructions.”
5. **39–49 s · 1977.** A gold RNA message leads from DNA to a ribosome. A colourful protein chain grows bead by bead; bacterium beams after it appears. Reads: 39–41 message; 41–45 protein synthesis; 45–48 finished chain. Match the chain into the insulin scene.
   Caption: “Life shares a genetic code. In 1977, engineered bacteria helped produce a human hormone.”
6. **49–60 s · 1978 → 1982.** Two insulin chains approach and link. Match to a labelled vial, with purification indicated by caption rather than suggesting raw bacteria are medicine. Bacterium holds a tiny heart beside it. Reads: 49–52 chains; 52–54 joining; 54–57 medicine approval; 57–59 final hopeful message; 59–60 fade to paper.
   Captions: “In 1978, bacteria helped make human insulin. In 1982, recombinant insulin was approved.” / “Purified and tested, it became life-saving medicine. Evolution’s tools. Human curiosity.”
